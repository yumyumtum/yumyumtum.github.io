Ferry basics

Bring the car aboard

  1. Drive to the Edmonds terminal early; summer lines can take time.
  2. Follow the crew onto the ferry and lock the car.
  3. Everyone can use the cabin, deck, and restrooms during the crossing.
  4. Return to the car before arriving in Kingston.

Three easy highlights

What the family can enjoy

Lake Crescent

A lake view, lodge, and restrooms. No waterfall hike needed.

Ruby + Kalaloch

Sea stacks at Ruby; more comfortable sitting time at sandy Kalaloch.

Hurricane Ridge

Beautiful car-accessible overlooks on a clear day; no hike required.

A comfortable plan for grandparents

Choose a hotel with an accessible or ground-floor room, walk-in shower, elevator, parking, and a short walk to breakfast. Ruby Beach’s path down is steep, so enjoy its overlook if needed. The mountain view depends on weather — it is always okay to skip it.

Booking & travel notes

What to do before you go

Reserve the hotel. The ferry and Olympic National Park do not require a timed reservation.

Ferry: no vehicle reservations

Edmonds ↔ Kingston loads cars first-come, first-served. On a summer weekday, arrive 45–60 minutes early; allow more time on Fridays and weekends. Wait in the vehicle line with water and snacks, and do not walk far from the car.

On board: lock the car, then use the cabin, deck, and restrooms. Return to the car before arrival.

Ferry: fare and return

For one standard car, two adults, two seniors, and two children, a current round-trip estimate is about $88; riders 18 and under are free. Edmonds collects the passenger and vehicle fare; Kingston collects the return vehicle fare. Recheck the official fare before travel.

Park and hotel

Olympic National Park has no timed entry. A $30 private-vehicle pass covers everyone in the car for seven days; use a grandparent’s Senior Pass if eligible. Reserve two nights in Port Angeles early, preferably an accessible or ground-floor room.
